The Silent Epidemic: Prescribing in High-Pressure Settings

Overburdened physicians, rapid consultations, and multi-drug polypharmacy lead to catastrophic yet preventable clinical events.

With 1 doctor per 1,500+ patients, clinicians see 80-120 patients per shift—leaving zero cognitive bandwidth to cross-check 5+ drug collisions.
~30%
Preventable Adverse Drug Reactions
Hospital admissions for Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) and GI bleeding repeatedly trace back to unmonitored NSAID and ACEi/ARB co-prescriptions.
The "Rolac" Crisis Ceiling Violation

Ketorolac (Rolac) is universally restricted to a strict 5-day ceiling by international & DGDA guidelines, yet routinely prescribed for 14-21 days for chronic joint pain, causing acute renal necrosis.

The "Triple Whammy" Renal Collapse

Co-prescribing an ACE-Inhibitor/ARB + Diuretic + NSAID in hypertensive or diabetic elders constricts afferent arterioles and plunges eGFR, precipitating rapid dialysis dependency.

Pediatric Vulnerability Reye's Syndrome

Aspirin (Ecosprin) dispensed to febrile children (<16 years) with viral influenza triggers Reye's syndrome—a rapid-onset encephalopathy and fatty liver failure with >40% mortality.

Source: Bangladesh National Health Bulletin & WHO Patient Safety Reports The Problem • High Volume, Polypharmacy, Zero Real-Time Checks
